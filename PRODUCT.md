# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

CFOs, Controllers, and Legal Directors at mid-to-large industrial companies in Nuevo León, Mexico. They are not technology buyers — they are compliance buyers. Their defining fear is a SEMARNAT fine or a SAT discrepancy that lands in their lap. They think in terms of legal exposure, audit seasons, and board accountability. They distrust anything that feels like a startup pitch, because they've been burned before.

## Product Purpose

SAAS Legal Tech ESG automates carbon footprint compliance for the industrial sector. It ingests SAT invoices and operational data and produces auditable Scope 1 and Scope 2 reports that are defensible in front of RENE inspectors, SAT auditors, and international clients requiring GHG Protocol compliance. The system is deterministic — rule-based, not AI-generated — so every calculation can be explained, traced, and defended. Success is measured by demo bookings from decision-makers, not traffic.

## Brand Personality

Authoritative. Exact. Unflinching.

The brand speaks the way a senior partner at a Big Four firm speaks in a board meeting: direct, precise, no filler. It does not use words like "innovador," "disruptivo," or "revolucionario." It doesn't comfort; it informs. Its credibility comes from specificity — real regulatory references, real fine thresholds, real normative citations — not from confidence theater.

## Anti-references

- Startup landing pages: HubSpot, Notion, any SaaS with confetti animations, gradient hero blobs, or a "rocket ship" metaphor.
- Consulting firms trying to look modern: McKinsey.com, Deloitte.com — not because they're bad, but because they signal "expensive black box", which is what this product replaces.
- Generic ESG platforms: Any site that leads with a leaf icon or a color gradient going from brown to green. This is not a sustainability story; it is a compliance story.
- Bloomberg Terminal aesthetics: The temptation is to replicate the terminal look. Avoid it — it reads as costume.

## Design Principles

1. **Every number earns its place.** The copy uses specific regulatory thresholds ($500,000 MXN multas, 25,000 t CO₂e RENE umbral) because vagueness signals uncertainty. No placeholder stats.
2. **Auditable all the way down.** The product's core promise is traceability. The design should feel like it operates the same way: every visual decision should have a reason, nothing decorative.
3. **Fear before relief.** The emotional arc of the page is: recognize the problem → feel the stakes → see the exit. Don't rush to the solution before the problem has landed.
4. **Business language, not engineering language.** The product audience signs compliance reports, not code commits. All copy speaks to accountability, not architecture.
5. **Serious is not boring.** Authority and restraint don't require visual austerity. The palette can be rich; the motion can be precise; the hierarchy can be bold. Serious means intentional, not timid.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Mexican Spanish throughout. Smooth scroll, keyboard navigation, and `:focus-visible` states required. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected via CSS. Touch targets 44×44px minimum on all interactive elements.
